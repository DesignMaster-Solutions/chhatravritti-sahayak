Hon'ble Pankaj Bhatia,J.
 


Heard learned counsel for the petitioner, learned Standing Counsel for respondent nos.1, 2, 4 & 5 and Shri Rinku Kumar Verma, Advocate holing brief of Shri Rakesh Kumar Chaudhary, learned counsel appearing for respondent no.6.

The present petition has been filed challenging the order dated 03.08.2018 (Annexure - 1).

The facts, in brief, are that in the year 2015, the petitioner took admission in M.B.A. First Year through State Entrance Examination and was allotted respondent no.6 - institution. The petitioner applied for online benefit of Post-matric Scholarship and Fee-reimbursement Scheme, 2015 - 16 through online mode. On 17.12.2015, respondent no.5 reported some discrepancy in the data of the petitioner as well as her sibling Shivam Singh studying in the same class on the ground that same income certificate was being used with different father's name. It is stated that the clarification was sought from respondent no.6 - institution, which was clarified by respondent no.6 on 28.12.2015 (Annexure - 4).

It is claimed that the brother of the petitioner was not being paid scholarship, thus, he preferred Writ Petition No.14553 (MS) of 2016 titled 'Shivam Singh v. State of U.P. & Ors,' in which directions were issued and the petitioner's brother was paid scholarship amounting to Rs.54,400/-; as the present petitioner was not paid scholarship, she preferred Writ Petition No.14551 (MS) of 2016 titled 'Divya Singh v. State of U.P. & Ors.', wherein directions were issued for taking a decision in respect of the petitioner. Subsequently, a contempt petition was also filed. In terms of the directions issued by this Court, the order impugned dated 03.08.2018 (Annexure - 1) was passed denying the scholarship to the petitioner.

A perusal of the said order reveals that while rejecting the scholarship of the petitioner, respondent no.2 was of the view that in view of same income certificate being used by the petitioner and her brother, certain clarifications were sought, which were not provided for, as such, the application was rejected.

Contention of learned counsel for the petitioner is that although requisite documents were provided by respondent no.6 as is evident from the perusal of Annexure - 4, however, on the basis of not providing the requisite clarification, the entitlement has been rejected.

Learned counsel for respondent no.6 argues that documents were provided as and when demanded.

Learned Standing Counsel justifies the order on the ground as taken in the order to the effect that the requisite clarifications were not supplied and also to the effect that as the academic year had come to an end coupled with the fact that the scholarship is provided on the basis of seniority list prepared, the relief cannot be granted to the petitioner.

A perusal of the order in question reveals that the ground taken that the clarification was not provided by respondent no.6, which on the face of it coupled with the reading of Annexure - 9 is wholly perverse. The second ground that the scholarships are payable on the basis of seniority and till availability of funds is also wholly arbitrary and illegal inasmuch as there is no concept of seniority provided under the scheme.

Considering the fact that there is no denial that the petitioner is entitled for scholarship, the impugned order dated 03.08.2018 (Annexure - 1) cannot be sustained and is quashed.

Respondent No.2 is directed to provide scholarship to the petitioner to which she was entitled in accordance with law within a period of two months from today.

The writ petition is allowed in above terms.

Learned Standing Counsel is directed to communicate this order to respondent no.2 for information and compliance.
